Open the right tab. Change a sentence. Say “the other one.” Voice Control lets ordinary speech or a typed inbox command drive the app already in front of you.

Walk through an exampleWhat’s in the build

Enable with --enable-voice-control in a DEBUG app. Native macOS Accessibility owns apps and your existing browser; no extension or CDP is required. Unique next steps run locally. Jev chooses only when more than one legal event is enabled. Confirm only pay, delete, or send. See the capability matrix and architecture. The illustrated scenes below are not a live microphone.

What exists in the experimental build.

A native panel takes typed instructions or local speech. Code classifies the interface (plain, suggestionPicker, datePicker) and lists legal events. One event executes locally. Several become one Jev Choice. Zero falls back to legality-filtered page controls. Your current browser session is used directly.

Local when the next step is unique

Allowlisted site opens, Google Flights form filling, unique city or calendar matches, overlay Escape, Search, YouTube/Maps/Wikipedia/web-search boxes, unique Gmail Compose, exact click/type/replace, and app activation. Jev never sees role=url destinations. Return is not enabled while a city or date overlay is open.

Jev when several events are legal

Competing unfocused city rows (London vs London, Ontario) are an outcome Choice over those ids plus insufficient_evidence / clarify. That is “where should this piece land,” not which key to mash. Unfamiliar in-page controls still use unconstrained Jev on a filtered target list. Jev does not authorize effects or declare the goal done.

Corrections and recovery

“Actually London” amends the retained goal. “The other one” uses recent alternatives or asks. Manual input pauses automation; Continue observes your change. Unknown effects cannot be blindly replayed. Several matching controls become a numbered list in the panel; on-screen number overlays remain later.

See what happened

Each turn writes one wide event (latest.md) plus joinable per-step records. Local logs may include the instruction and control labels; Copy diagnostics omits them. Field values, selected text, audio, screenshots, and remote Jev bodies stay out.

What you’ll be able to do.

A full voice-control experience, built in stages. Expand a feature to see what you could say and how it should behave.

Stage labels describe the proposed order, not dates or release commitments. Support will be qualified by app and action.

Two ways to speak.

Use a quick command while your hands are on the keyboard, or start a visible hands-free session. Your normal dictation shortcut keeps doing dictation.

Hold key

Hold to talk

Hold a dedicated, configurable shortcut, speak, then release to act. The current build shows available text previews; highlighted target previews in the illustrations remain proposed.

Start a hands-free session

Turn on Voice Control explicitly. A visible listening indicator stays with you as you issue commands and follow-ups. No held key is required.

Hands-free means a session you started. Always-on background listening is outside this proposal.

“Stop”

Halt queued actions and pause the task. Keep the active session ready for a correction.

“Cancel”

Discard the pending task and its approvals.

“Stop listening”

Turn off the microphone and pause the task. Choose Continue explicitly when ready to resume.

Typing mode has its own visible escape command so words like “stop” can remain literal text. A physical Stop control stays available.

A useful first release.
A much broader destination.

The DEBUG build already runs Stage 1’s local routes and the Flights form machine. A complete Google Flights results run is still the acceptance bar. Broader editing and cross-app drafting remain future work.

Stage 1

Direct control

The everyday actions you repeat all day.

  • Apps, windows and supported browser tabs
  • Clicking, menus and bounded scrolling
  • Exact text insertion
  • Hold and hands-free invocation
  • Target choices, basic correction and help
  • Short “do this, then that” sequences
Stage 2

Deeper control

Work precisely with your words and your Mac.

  • Phrase and paragraph editing
  • Spoken rewrites and translation
  • Richer references and scoped undo
  • Persistent typing and spelling refinement
  • System and media controls
  • Broader app-specific behavior
Stage 3

Larger tasks

Carry a clear goal across several steps.

  • Forms and richer file actions
  • Read and move content between apps
  • Draft replies from selected material
  • MacParakeet library workflows
  • Bounded, visible task progress
  • Optional visual targeting for difficult controls

Initial app candidates: TextEdit, Notes, Finder, Safari and Chrome. Browser control uses native Accessibility without an extension. Mail, Slack and editors need their own compatibility checks.

Jev chooses only when it must.

Code lists legal events from the current Accessibility snapshot. Unique events skip the model. Jev is a judge: one Choice among enabled ids, or among legality-filtered controls when no domain machine applies.

Your Mac hears and acts

Speech stays local. MacParakeet observes Accessibility, classifies the situation, executes one action, and checks a fresh snapshot. Allowlisted URLs and form values never come from the model.

Jev picks among enabled events

When two city rows both match “London,” Jev receives those event ids, the goal, and the situation — not a blank page. It can also say the evidence is insufficient. It never proves the goal is done.

A text model writes when needed

“Make this friendlier” uses your configured rewrite provider under separate consent. There is no generative worker in the click loop. TTS and the Jev CLI are later.

Local audio. Explicit cloud use.

Your voice stays on your Mac. Jev is a cloud service: the command and relevant interface text are sent only after you enable that feature. Selected-text writing has separate provider consent. Visible field values can contain selected words under general Jev context consent; redacting the dedicated selection property is not a promise to hide all visible text.

Fast has to mean the whole experience.

Our five early text-only calls took 216–293 ms. That is promising, but excludes speech recognition, finding controls and checking the result. Real voice-to-action latency still needs testing.

The details that make it usable.

What happens when it picks the wrong thing?

The experimental build retains task context for corrections such as “Actually London” and “the other one.” Several matching controls become a numbered list in the panel; say the number. On-screen overlays and broad tab referents are later. Native text Undo applies only when the previous edit and current value still match.

Will it ask permission before every click?

No. Supported navigation and ordinary low-risk actions should run after you finish the command. Ambiguity triggers a targeted question. Sending, buying, publishing or deleting requires confirmation of the concrete effect. Unknown consequences may also require confirmation.

What about typing words that sound like commands?

“Type the words click send” inserts those words. A one-shot text command owns the rest of that utterance. Persistent typing is a distinct visible mode, with a small, explicit escape grammar. The current escapes are “command mode” to leave literal mode and “command stop” to pause. Spelling mode remains proposed.

Can it do several things in one sentence?

Yes. Unique steps such as opening Flights, filling Zurich, and pressing a unique city run locally, each against a fresh snapshot. When several cities remain, that single step is a Jev Choice. Broader cross-app drafting is still later. “Type research and development” stays one text insertion.

What if the app changes, the network drops, or I take over?

It invalidates old target authority and pauses when you take over. Continue observes your changes while preserving the task. Activity distinguishes verified effects, observed transitions and unknown results. If Jev is unavailable, only supported local commands remain available; it will not quietly switch providers or repeat a possibly completed submission.

What are we deliberately not planning?

No always-on ambient listener, unrestricted shell execution, or unattended remote agent. No promise that every app or unlabeled control will work. The full feature is broad, but it remains a visible, user-invoked way to control your own Mac.
